# Questions by stage

Our own question sets for reviewing an AI tool before it is used with people with CF. Answer each one from something you can point to: a document, a demo, or a test you ran. If you cannot point to anything, the answer is **unknown**.

## 1. Design: who made it, and for whom

- Who built the tool, and who pays for it?
- Were people with CF, families and CF clinicians involved in designing it? In what role, and when?
- What is it meant to do, in the maker's own words? What does the maker say it must not be used for?
- Who is it for: patients and families, clinicians, or both? Does it say so plainly?
- Is it meant to support a person's own decisions and their talks with the care team, or to replace a step a person does today?

## 2. Development: what it learned from, and how it was tested

- What data trained or guides it? Is that stated?
- How well does that data represent people with CF: children and adults, different languages, regions, incomes and levels of access to care?
- Was it tested on CF questions? By whom, on what, and are the results published?
- Does it show the sources behind an answer, so a person can check them?
- What does it do when it does not know: say so, or guess?

## 3. Deployment: how output reaches the person

- How does an answer reach the person: directly, or through a clinician first?
- Is consent asked before a person's information is used? In plain words?
- What do the terms say about storing chats, reusing them (for example to train the tool), sharing them, and for how long?
- Can a person export their data, and delete it? Have you tried?
- Does it ask for identifiers (names, dates of birth, record numbers) it does not need?
- Does it work in the languages, reading levels and devices your community uses? What does it cost the person?

## 4. Monitoring: how mistakes are caught

- How are errors and made-up content watched for, and by whom?
- Who can see the reports of mistakes: the maker, your team, the people using it?
- How do people report a problem, and what happens next?
- How will you know if the tool changes (a new version, new terms)?

## 5. Evaluation: whether it helps

- Does it help or weaken human decisions and relationships, such as the person's own judgement and their talks with the care team?
- Could it lead people to rely on it instead of asking a person?
- What happens when it is wrong, and who is responsible?
- How do you stop using it, and what happens to the data then?
- When will you review it again?

## Red flags

Any of these is a reason to stop and discuss before going further:

- It asks for identifiers it does not need.
- It claims to diagnose, give doses or decide eligibility.
- There is no way to see the sources behind an answer.
- There is no way to export or delete your data.
- There is no plain statement of what happens to chats.
- It makes marketing claims with no evidence behind them.
