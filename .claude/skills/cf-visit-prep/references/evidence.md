# Evidence behind this skill

Why each rule is here, and how much stands behind it. Sources were searched and read on 2026-10-08. Most of this skill is a way of organising questions, so most rows are our judgement.

- **Sourced rows** give the source, a short quote and an honest strength. Each quote was checked by script as an exact copy of the text we saved from the source, and a person read it against the row. A quote is a pointer: read the source itself before you rely on it.
- **"Our judgement, not a finding"** marks a rule we chose because it is good practice. It is not a fact about the world.
- **"No source found in our 2026-10-08 search"** means we looked and found nothing we could read at the source. The skill does not state that statement as a fact. Not finding a source does not prove the opposite.

Strength: **strong** = read at the source and says what the row says; **limited** = only an abstract that a fetch tool partly paraphrased, only part of a page, or a general study applied to CF; **none** = no source.

| Rule or statement | What supports it | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Never answer the medical question; turn it into a question for the care team | Our judgement, not a finding: the care team answers medical questions. Related: in a study with 1,298 participants, people using chatbots on medical scenarios did "both no better than the control group". *Clinical knowledge in LLMs does not translate to human interactions*, arXiv preprint, 2025, [arxiv.org/abs/2504.18919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18919) | Limited: abstract read at the source, but a general study, not about CF |
| Never ask for names, dates of birth, record numbers or places | Our judgement, not a finding. In line with the APA's advice to "avoid entering personally identifiable details". *APA Health Advisory on the Use of Generative AI Chatbots and Wellness Applications for Mental Health*, American Psychological Association, 2025, [PDF](https://www.apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-ai-chatbots-wellness-apps-mental-health.pdf) | Strong for what the APA advises (read at the source); the advisory is about mental health, not CF |
| Bring the names of any AI tools or apps used for health questions | The APA advisory (above): "It is strongly recommended that users share what GenAI tools or wellness apps they are using with their health providers." | Strong: read at the source; about mental health, not CF |
| Top three questions, the person's own words, an optional one-page summary, a what-to-bring list | Our judgement, not a finding: a way to organise a visit. We did not search for studies of question lists for clinic visits | None (a rule) |
| Say plainly when something should not wait for the visit | Our judgement, not a finding: safety comes before the task | None (a rule) |
| A person who sounds low is pointed to people, never to the assistant | The APA advisory (above): chatbots and wellness apps "should not be used as a replacement for a qualified mental health care provider" | Strong: read at the source; about mental health, not CF |
| Leave a blank for phone numbers, web addresses and insurance rules | No source found in our 2026-10-08 search on made-up phone numbers or insurance advice errors. Kept as a rule: our judgement, not a finding | None |
